# Export Potential of Brazilian Sugar to the Middle East

## Executive Summary

The Middle East represents a structurally attractive market for Brazilian sugar exports, driven by demographic growth, food security imperatives, and limited domestic production capacity in most Gulf and Levant markets. Brazil's position as the world's largest sugar exporter, combined with competitive logistics and established trade relationships, positions it well—though competition from India, Thailand, and the EU, along with policy and logistics frictions, warrants careful strategic assessment.

---

## Market Fundamentals

**Demand drivers:**
- Regional sugar consumption is supported by population growth, high per-capita consumption (notably in Gulf states), food processing industries, and hospitality/confectionery sectors.
- Several Middle Eastern countries are net importers with minimal domestic cane or beet production (e.g., UAE, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Qatar, Jordan, Lebanon, Yemen).
- Strategic food security programs—particularly Saudi Arabia's and the UAE's—create demand for reliable, diversified supply partners.

**Key import markets to prioritize:**
1. **Saudi Arabia** – largest Gulf importer; refining and re-export hub ambitions.
2. **UAE** – major re-export platform (Jebel Ali, Dubai) serving wider MENA and East Africa.
3. **Egypt** – large consumer but a sensitive market: domestic beet/cane production plus periodic import needs; price-sensitive and policy-managed.
4. **Algeria, Morocco, Tunisia**
