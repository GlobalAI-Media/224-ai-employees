**Market Analysis: Brazilian Sugar Exports to the Middle East**

**Executive Summary**

The Middle East represents a strategically significant and structurally attractive market for Brazilian sugar exports. Brazil, as the world's largest sugar exporter, is well-positioned to serve the region's growing demand, driven by food security priorities, population growth, and limited domestic production capacity. However, success requires navigating price competition, logistical complexities, and geopolitical volatility.

---

**1. Market Fundamentals**

- **Demand profile:** The Middle East and North Africa (MENA) region is a major net sugar importer, with consumption driven by food processing, beverage production, and household demand. Key markets include Egypt, Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Iran, Iraq, and Algeria.
- **Production constraints:** Arable land and water scarcity limit domestic sugar production across most of the region, sustaining structural import dependence.
- **Growth drivers:** Population growth, urbanization, food security strategies (e.g., Saudi Vision 2030, UAE food security initiatives), and expanding food and beverage manufacturing.

---

**2. Brazil's Competitive Position**

- **Scale and reliability:** Brazil accounts for roughly half of global raw sugar trade, offering year-round supply and consistent quality.
- **Cost competitiveness:** Efficient sugarcane production and port infrastructure (Santos, Paranaguá) support competitive pricing.
- **Product range:** Brazil can supply raw sugar (for refining) and refined/ICUMSA 45 sugar (for direct consumption), matching diverse buyer needs.
