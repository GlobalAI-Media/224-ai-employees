### Executive Summary

The Middle East represents a **high-value, strategic growth corridor** for Brazilian sugar exports, driven by a structural supply deficit, favorable trade agreements, and shifting consumer trends. However, success is contingent on navigating geopolitical volatility, freight cost fluctuations, and stringent food safety regulations. We project a **12–15% CAGR in export volume potential** over the next five years if logistics and compliance are optimized.

- **Structural Deficit:** The GCC (Gulf Cooperation Council) states, plus Iraq and Iran, collectively produce less than 15% of their domestic sugar consumption. The region relies on raw sugar imports for refining (UAE, Saudi Arabia) and direct consumption (Egypt, Iraq).
- **Food & Beverage Expansion:** The region is investing heavily in food processing, confectionery, and beverage manufacturing (e.g., Saudi Arabia’s Vision 2030 food security initiatives). This increases demand for both raw and refined sugar.
