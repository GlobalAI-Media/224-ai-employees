# Export Potential Analysis: Brazilian Sugar to the Middle East

## Executive Summary

The Middle East represents one of the most promising destinations for Brazilian sugar exports, driven by structural supply deficits, growing food-processing industries, and established trade relationships. Brazil, as the world's largest sugar producer and exporter, is well-positioned to capitalize on this demand. However, success will depend on navigating logistics, pricing competition, and regional policy dynamics.

---

## 1. Market Fundamentals

**Demand Drivers in the Middle East:**
- **High per-capita consumption** in Gulf states (UAE, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait) due to food processing, beverages, and confectionery industries
- **Refining capacity gaps** — many MENA countries import raw sugar for domestic refining
- **Population growth** and urbanization driving processed food demand
- **Strategic stockpiling** by Gulf governments for food security

**Key Importers:**
| Country | Profile |
|---------|---------|
| Saudi Arabia | Largest regional importer; both raw and refined |
| UAE | Re-export hub (Jebel Ali, Khalifa Port) |
| Egypt | Major importer, though domestic beet production rising |
| Iraq, Yemen, Syria | Consistent raw sugar demand |
| Iran | Historically significant buyer (sanctions-sensitive) |
