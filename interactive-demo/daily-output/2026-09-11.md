**Market Analysis: Export Potential of Brazilian Sugar to the Middle East**

**Executive Summary**

The Middle East represents a strategically significant and structurally attractive market for Brazilian sugar exports. With limited domestic agricultural capacity for sugar production, strong population growth, and deep-rooted cultural demand for sugar in food and beverage consumption, the region offers Brazil a durable export destination. Brazil is already the world's largest sugar exporter, and the Middle East is among its top regional buyers. The potential is high, though subject to price volatility, logistics considerations, and competition from India and Thailand.

---

**1. Market Fundamentals in the Middle East**

**Demand drivers:**
- **Low self-sufficiency:** Most Gulf and Levantine countries cannot produce sugar at scale due to climate and water constraints. Saudi Arabia, UAE, Kuwait, Jordan, and Yemen are heavily import-dependent.
- **Demographic and income growth:** Population growth and rising incomes support consumption in processed foods, confectionery, and beverages.
- **Cultural consumption patterns:** Sugar is integral to regional diets—tea, Arabic coffee, desserts, and hospitality traditions sustain baseline demand.
- **Food security priorities:** Gulf states have prioritized stable food import supply chains, creating opportunities for reliable, long-term suppliers.

**Refining capacity:** Several countries (e.g., Saudi Arabia, UAE, Egypt) operate refineries that import raw sugar, while others import refined sugar directly—giving Brazil flexibility across both raw and refined product segments.
