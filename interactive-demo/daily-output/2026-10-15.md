**Market Analysis: Export Potential for Brazilian Sugar in the Middle East**

**Executive Summary**

The Middle East represents a strategically significant and structurally attractive market for Brazilian sugar exports. While the region presents meaningful opportunities driven by demographic growth, food security priorities, and refining capacity gaps, exporters must navigate price sensitivity, competitive pressures from India and Thailand, and evolving trade policies. Overall, the market warrants prioritization within a diversified export strategy.

---

**1. Market Fundamentals**

- **Demand Profile:** Middle Eastern nations are among the world's highest per-capita sugar consumers, driven by population growth, food processing industries, and cultural consumption patterns (beverages, confectionery, traditional sweets).
- **Import Dependence:** Most GCC and Levant countries lack arable land and water resources for competitive domestic production, making them structurally import-reliant. Key importers include Egypt, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Iraq, Algeria, and Yemen.
- **Volume:** The MENA region collectively imports several million tonnes annually, representing a substantial share of global raw and white sugar trade.

**2. Brazil's Competitive Position**

- **Supply Reliability:** Brazil is the world's largest sugar exporter, with a mature logistics chain (Santos, Paranaguá, Recife ports) and the capacity to serve large-volume contracts year-round.
- **Product Range:** Brazil can supply both raw sugar (for refining markets like Algeria and Egypt) and white/refined sugar (for direct consumption markets).
