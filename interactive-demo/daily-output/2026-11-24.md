**Market Analysis: Potential for Brazilian Sugar Exports to the Middle East**

**Executive Summary**

The Middle East represents a structurally attractive, high-potential market for Brazilian sugar exports. The region combines limited domestic production capacity, strong and growing consumption driven by food processing and beverage industries, and a strategic geographic position as a re-export hub. Brazil, as the world's largest sugar exporter with unmatched cost competitiveness and logistics scale, is well positioned to expand its share—particularly in refined and VHP (very high polarization) grades—though price competition from India, Thailand, and the UAE's own refining capacity warrants careful commercial strategy.

---

**1. Demand Fundamentals**

- **Consumption profile:** Per-capita sugar consumption in Gulf states is among the highest globally (GCC average often cited above 30 kg/capita/year), driven by confectionery, bakery, dairy, and beverage sectors, plus traditional consumption patterns.
- **Demand growth drivers:** Population growth, urbanization, food-processing localization (e.g., Saudi Arabia's food-security agenda under Vision 2030), and tourism/hospitality expansion (UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar).
- **Import dependence:** Most GCC countries produce negligible sugar and rely on imports—Saudi Arabia, UAE, Egypt, and Algeria are among the region's largest importers, with Egypt also a major domestic producer and occasional exporter.

**2. Competitive Landscape**

- **India and Thailand:** Primary price competitors, particularly for raw and white sugar
