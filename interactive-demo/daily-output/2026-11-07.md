# Export Potential: Brazilian Sugar to the Middle East

## Market Context

The Middle East is one of the world's most structurally import-dependent sugar regions. GCC countries (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman), plus Egypt, Iran, Iraq, and Levant markets, collectively import several million tonnes annually, driven by food processing, beverage production, confectionery, and household consumption. Domestic production (notably Egypt, Iran, and Saudi beet refining) covers only a fraction of demand.

Brazil is the world's largest sugar exporter and the natural default supplier, alongside Thailand, India, and the EU. The Middle East typically absorbs a meaningful share of Brazil's raw sugar exports, with Saudi Arabia, Egypt, UAE, and Iraq among recurring destinations.

## Key Demand Drivers

- **Raw sugar for refining:** Saudi Arabia, UAE, and Egypt operate refineries configured for Brazilian raw (high-polarity VHP/VVHP). Egypt's state tenders (GASC/ESIC) are a recurring, price-sensitive outlet.
- **Industrial demand:** Confectionery, bakery, and beverage sectors (e.g., GCC bottling plants) require consistent quality and reliable logistics.
- **Halal and quality compliance:** Brazilian sugar generally meets Gulf standards; certification and documentation discipline are differentiators.
- **Population and tourism growth:** GCC food-service expansion and pilgrimage-related consumption support baseline demand.
