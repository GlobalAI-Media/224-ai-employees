### Executive Summary
The Middle East presents a **high-potential, high-value market** for Brazilian sugar, driven by a structural supply deficit, geographic proximity (via the Red Sea/Gulf), and a shift toward refined and specialty sugars. However, success requires navigating complex trade protocols, price volatility, and regional geopolitical risks. We recommend a phased entry strategy focusing on the UAE and Saudi Arabia as primary hubs.

---

### 1. Market Demand & Structure
- **Supply Deficit:** The GCC region imports ~90% of its sugar needs. Domestic production is negligible due to arid climate. Demand is projected to grow at 2.5-3% CAGR through 2030, driven by population growth and the expansion of food & beverage (F&B) and confectionery industries.
- **Key Buyers:** 
  - **Industrial:** Refineries in UAE (e.g., Al Khaleej Sugar) and Saudi Arabia (e.g., Saudi Sugar Company) import raw sugar for refining.
  - **Consumer:** Growing demand for white refined sugar, organic, and low-glycemic variants (e.g., demerara, muscovado) in premium retail sectors.
- **Re-export Hub:** The UAE re-exports up to 30%
