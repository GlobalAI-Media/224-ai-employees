**Market Analysis: Export Potential for Brazilian Sugar in the Middle East**

**Executive Summary**

The Middle East represents a strategically significant and structurally attractive market for Brazilian sugar exports. The region combines high per-capita consumption, limited domestic production capacity, and a favorable trade architecture for Brazilian suppliers. Brazil, as the world's largest sugar exporter, is well positioned to deepen its footprint. However, the opportunity is not without competitive and logistical considerations.

---

**1. Market Fundamentals**

- **Demand profile:** The Middle East and North Africa (MENA) region consumes roughly 10–12 million tonnes of sugar annually, with GCC states among the highest per-capita consumers globally (refined sugar use in beverages, confectionery, and food processing).
- **Self-sufficiency gap:** Only a handful of countries (notably Egypt, Morocco, Iran, and Saudi Arabia to a limited degree) produce sugar domestically. The Gulf states—UAE, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain, Oman—are almost entirely import-dependent.
- **Refining capacity:** The UAE and Saudi Arabia have invested in large-scale refineries (e.g., Al Khaleej Sugar in Dubai, which is among the world's largest standalone refineries). This creates demand for both **raw sugar** for local refining and **refined/white sugar** for direct consumption.
