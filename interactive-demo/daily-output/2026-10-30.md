**Market Analysis: Export Potential of Brazilian Sugar to the Middle East**

**Executive Summary**

The Middle East represents a structurally attractive destination for Brazilian sugar exports, driven by supply deficits, demographic growth, and established trade corridors. Brazil, as the world's largest sugar exporter, is well-positioned to capture share—particularly in refined and VHP (very high polarization) grades—though success depends on navigating freight economics, competition from India and Thailand, and regional policy shifts. Overall potential is **moderate-to-high**, with the strongest opportunities in Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) markets and Egypt.

---

**1. Demand-Side Fundamentals**

- **Structural import dependence:** Most Middle Eastern countries lack arable land and water for competitive sugar production. The region imports the majority of its consumption, making it a natural target for major exporters.
- **Demographics and consumption growth:** Population growth, urbanization, and food-processing expansion (beverages, confectionery, bakery) support steady demand growth of roughly 2–3% annually in key markets.
- **Key markets:** Egypt (largest, but with domestic beet industry and periodic import controls), Saudi Arabia, UAE, Iraq, Yemen, Syria, Jordan, and the GCC states. Saudi Arabia and the UAE are the most commercially accessible and highest-value per capita.
- **Halal and quality requirements:** Brazilian sugar meets regional standards, and Brazil's established halal certification capacity for food exports is an advantage.
