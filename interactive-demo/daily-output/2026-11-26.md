# Export Potential of Brazilian Sugar to the Middle East

## Executive Summary
The Middle East represents a strategically significant and structurally growing market for Brazilian sugar exports. Brazil, as the world's largest sugar exporter, is well-positioned to serve the region's expanding demand driven by population growth, food processing industrialization, and re-export hubs. However, competitive pressure from India, Thailand, and the UAE's refining capacity, alongside logistical and geopolitical factors, requires a differentiated market-entry strategy.

## 1. Market Fundamentals

**Demand Drivers**
- **Population and consumption growth:** The MENA region imports roughly 70–80% of its sugar needs. GCC countries (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Kuwait, Qatar, Oman, Bahrain) are among the world's highest per-capita sugar consumers, largely due to confectionery, beverage, and bakery industries.
- **Food processing expansion:** Saudi Arabia's Vision 2030 industrial diversification and the UAE's food-manufacturing hub ambitions sustain refined and raw sugar demand.
- **Re-export and refining hubs:** The UAE (Al Khaleej Sugar, Emirates Refining) and Saudi Arabia operate major refineries that import raw sugar for regional redistribution—a natural fit for Brazilian raw sugar.
- **Halal and food-security alignment:** Brazil's established halal certification ecosystem (ABNT NBR 16284) strengthens its credibility as a supplier to Islamic markets.
