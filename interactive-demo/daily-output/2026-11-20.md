**Market Analysis: Brazilian Sugar Exports to the Middle East**

**Executive Summary**

The Middle East represents a structurally attractive, high-potential market for Brazilian sugar exports. The region combines limited domestic production capacity, rising consumption tied to population growth and food/beverage industrialization, and a strategic geographic position as a re-export hub. Brazil, as the world's largest sugar exporter, is well-positioned to capture share—particularly in raw sugar for refining—though competition from India, Thailand, and the EU, along with logistical and tariff considerations, warrants a segmented approach.

---

**1. Market Fundamentals**

- **Demand profile:** The Middle East and North Africa (MENA) region imports a substantial share of its sugar needs. Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) states—Saudi Arabia, UAE, Kuwait, Qatar, Oman, Bahrain—are net importers with high per-capita consumption. Egypt, Iran, Iraq, and Algeria add scale but carry greater volatility (FX constraints, sanctions, payment risk).
- **Refining capacity:** Several Gulf states operate refineries (e.g., Al Khaleej in Dubai, Saudi refineries) that favor **raw sugar (ICUMSA 800–1200)** as feedstock. This aligns well with Brazil's export profile, where raw sugar dominates volumes.
