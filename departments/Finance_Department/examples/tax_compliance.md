### Executive Summary

As Global AI Media Group expands its AI Workforce as a Service (AIWaaS) across Southeast Asia, we must address multi-jurisdictional tax risks inherent in cross-border service delivery, intellectual property (IP) licensing, and the classification of AI employees as "services" vs. "digital goods." Below is a structured compliance framework for our operations in key markets (Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, Vietnam, Philippines).

---

### 1. Transfer Pricing (TP) – Arm’s Length Profit Allocation

Our AI employees operate from centralized servers (likely Singapore or Malaysia) but generate revenue in each client jurisdiction. TP documentation must justify profit attribution.

| **TP Risk Area** | **Consideration** | **Recommended Approach** |
|------------------|-------------------|---------------------------|
| **Intercompany Service Fees** | AI employee "lease" fees charged to local subsidiaries or direct clients. | Use **Transactional Net Margin Method (TNMM)** with a cost-plus markup of **8–12%** on operating costs (server, AI training, support). Benchmark against comparable IT/BPO service providers. |
| **IP Royalties** | Proprietary AI algorithms and models used in service delivery. | License IP from Singapore (holding company) to operating entities at a **5–7% royalty** on gross revenue, supported by a functional analysis showing Singapore as the IP development hub. |
| **Profit Split** | If local entities perform significant client customization. | Apply a **Residual Profit Split** – allocate routine returns (cost-plus) to local entities, residual profit to IP owner. |
| **Documentation** | All SE Asian countries require contemporaneous TP documentation (e.g., Thailand’s Revenue Code Section 71 bis). | Prepare **Master File** (global) and **Local Files** for each jurisdiction with >THB 200M revenue. Use OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines as base. |

**Key Risk:** Tax authorities (e.g., Indonesia DGT, Vietnam GDT) may recharacterize AI leasing as a **royalty** (subject to withholding tax) rather than a service fee. Mitigate via clear contractual language and functional analysis.

---

### 2. VAT/GST – Cross-Border Digital Services

AI workforce leasing is classified as **electronic services** under most SE Asian VAT/GST regimes. Our liability depends on client type (B2B vs. B2C) and server location.

| **Jurisdiction** | **B2B Treatment** | **B2C Treatment** | **Rate** | **Registration Threshold** |
|------------------|-------------------|-------------------|----------|----------------------------|
| **Singapore** | Reverse charge applies to imported services (client accounts for GST). | No GST if foreign supplier (us) does not have a local presence. | 9% | >SGD 1M global revenue (likely triggered) |
| **Malaysia** | Service recipient must self-account for SST (6% on service fees). | No SST on imported digital services (yet). | 6% | >MYR 500K (only if local presence) |
| **Thailand** | VAT withheld by client (7%) – we issue tax invoice. | No VAT if no local PE. | 7% | >THB 1.8M (if local PE) |
| **Indonesia** | **VAT on imported digital services** – client must withhold and remit **11% PPN** on our invoice. | Same as B2B – no distinction. | 11% | >IDR 600M (if designated as digital platform) |
| **Vietnam** | **Foreign Contractor Tax (FCT)** – combined VAT (5%) + CIT (5%) = **10%** on gross service fees, unless treaty relief. | Same as B2B. | 10% (FCT) | No threshold – applies from first transaction |
| **Philippines** | **12% VAT** on digital services – client withholds and remits. | 12% VAT if we have a local agent. | 12% | >PHP 3M (if local presence) |

**Action Plan:**
- **For Vietnam & Indonesia:** Register for FCT/PPN compliance via local tax representative or client withholding. Negotiate contracts to be **gross-up** or **net of tax**.
- **For Singapore:** Register for GST voluntarily (even if B2C) to claim input tax on server costs.
- **For all others:** Ensure invoices clearly state "AI Workforce Service – Non-VATable" where reverse charge applies.
