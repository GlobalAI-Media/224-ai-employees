**I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

We have reviewed the key clauses of the Software-as-a-Service (“SaaS”) Agreement for the leasing of AI workforce modules (the “Agreement”) provided by the Vendor. The Agreement is broadly favorable to the Vendor and contains several material risks to Global AI Media Group (“Client”).

The most critical exposures are: (1) an overly broad indemnification clause that makes Client liable for Vendor’s negligence; (2) ambiguous IP ownership of AI-generated output and training data; and (3) a unilateral termination-for-convenience clause that creates operational dependency without adequate exit runway. Data privacy provisions are partially compliant with GDPR but fail to address cross-border transfer mechanisms under the new EU-US Data Privacy Framework.

We recommend renegotiation of Clauses 7.2, 8.1, and 12.4 as a condition precedent to execution.

---

**II. CLAUSE-BY-CLAUSE RISK ASSESSMENT**

**Clause 7.2 – Limitation of Liability (Cap and Exclusions)**
- **Risk Rating: HIGH**
- **Analysis:** The clause caps Vendor’s aggregate liability at 100% of fees paid in the preceding 12 months. More critically, it excludes liability for “indirect, consequential, or loss of profits” without a carve-out for data breaches or gross negligence. Given that the AI workforce will handle client data and make operational decisions, a data breach or algorithmic error causing business interruption would leave Client with minimal recovery.
- **Regulatory Reference:** Under GDPR Art. 82, liability for data processing is joint and several. This contractual cap may not be enforceable against regulatory fines, but it severely limits commercial recovery.
- **Recommendation:** (a) Increase cap to 300% of annual fees; (b) carve out liability for breach of confidentiality, data protection obligations, and gross negligence; (c) add a mutual indemnity for third-party IP infringement claims arising from the AI’s output.

**Clause 8.1 – Intellectual Property Ownership**
- **Risk Rating: HIGH**
- **Analysis:** The clause states that Vendor retains all rights to the AI platform, algorithms, and “any outputs generated by the AI workforce.” It grants Client a limited, non-exclusive, non-transferable license to use outputs “for internal business purposes.” This is a critical flaw: if the AI generates proprietary code, marketing copy, or strategic analyses, Client does not own those deliverables. This defeats the purpose of leasing an AI workforce.
- **Recommendation:** (a) Amend to state that all AI-generated outputs are “works made for hire” owned exclusively by Client; (b) Vendor retains only the underlying pre-trained models; (c) include a warranty that outputs do not infringe third-party IP; (d) if Vendor refuses, negotiate a perpetual, irrevocable, royalty-free license to use outputs for any purpose.

**Clause 9.3 – Data Privacy and Processing**
- **Risk Rating: MEDIUM**
- **Analysis:** The clause commits Vendor to GDPR compliance and lists processing purposes. However, it lacks: (a) a Data Processing Agreement (DPA) with Art. 28 terms; (b) specific sub-processor list and objection rights; (c) data breach notification within 24 hours; (d) a clear mechanism for data deletion upon termination. It also fails to address UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 45/2021 on Personal Data Protection (PDPL) for our regional operations.
- **Recommendation:** (a) Attach a full DPA as an exhibit; (b) require Vendor to certify compliance with EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework or provide SCCs for transfers; (c) add a clause for data residency in the UAE/EU as applicable; (d) mandate deletion of all Client data within 30 days post-termination with written certification.
